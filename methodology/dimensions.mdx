## Dimension Framework

Each dimension measures a distinct aspect of AI governance. Together, they provide a comprehensive view of how a company governs artificial intelligence.

### D1: Leadership & Accountability

**Measures:** Board oversight structure, C-suite AI ownership, named responsibility chains, governance team composition, strategic alignment, safety culture.

**Committee:** Full Board | **Standards:** ISO 42001 Clause 5, NIST GOVERN 1.1-1.7, EU AI Act Article 4

Key indicators: Named C-suite AI accountability, board AI competency and composition, board agenda integration, committee charter language, safety culture signals.

---

### D2: AI Safety & Robustness

**Measures:** Testing protocols, red-teaming, deployment safeguards, incident response, responsible scaling, governance under stress.

**Committee:** Risk | **Standards:** ISO 42001 Annex B.6, NIST MEASURE 2.5-2.13, EU AI Act Articles 9, 15

Key indicators: Pre-deployment safety testing, staged rollout protocols, responsible scaling policies, dual-use testing, agentic AI governance, defence-in-depth architecture.

**Highest-value signal:** Governance Under Stress (2I), which measures documented instances where governance constrained commercial decisions.

---

### D3: Transparency & Disclosure

**Measures:** Public reporting on AI systems, model documentation, risk factor disclosure, stakeholder communication.

**Committee:** Audit | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 13, ISO 42001 Annex B.7, NIST GOVERN 4

Key indicators: AI system inventory disclosure, model card or system card publication, risk factor specificity in SEC filings, algorithmic impact assessments.

---

### D4: Data Ethics & Privacy

**Measures:** Training data governance, consent frameworks, privacy-by-design, data quality controls, cross-border compliance.

**Committee:** Risk | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 10, NIST MEASURE 2.6, ISO 42001 Annex B.8

Key indicators: Training data documentation, consent and licensing frameworks, privacy impact assessments, data quality and bias testing protocols.

---

### D5: Governance Infrastructure

**Measures:** Policies, processes, organizational architecture, internal audit, change management, resource allocation.

**Committee:** Nominating/Governance | **Standards:** ISO 42001 Clauses 6-10, NIST GOVERN 1.2-1.5

Key indicators: AI governance policy existence and specificity, internal audit of AI systems, change management protocols, dedicated governance resources, model risk management framework.

---

### D6: Fairness & Bias Mitigation

**Measures:** Bias testing, demographic impact analysis, fairness metrics, remediation processes, accessibility.

**Committee:** Compensation/HR | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 10(2), NIST MEASURE 2.6

Key indicators: Bias testing methodology, demographic impact reporting, fairness metric definition and monitoring, third-party bias audits, remediation evidence.

---

### D7: Societal, Workforce & Environmental Impact

**Measures:** Workforce transition planning, environmental footprint of AI compute, community impact, responsible deployment in sensitive contexts.

**Committee:** Compensation/HR | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 14.4, NIST GOVERN 6.1-6.2

Key indicators: Workforce displacement planning, reskilling investment, compute carbon footprint reporting, community engagement on AI deployment, responsible use policies.

---

### D8: Regulatory & Compliance Posture

**Measures:** Regulatory preparedness, compliance program maturity, cross-jurisdictional alignment, proactive engagement with regulators.

**Committee:** Audit | **Standards:** EU AI Act (full compliance), NIST AI RMF GOVERN

Key indicators: Regulatory mapping and gap analysis, compliance team composition, cross-jurisdictional preparedness, regulatory engagement (comment letters, consultations), conformity assessment readiness.

---

### D9: Cybersecurity & AI Resilience

**Measures:** AI-specific security controls, adversarial robustness, supply chain security, incident detection and response.

**Committee:** Risk | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 15, NIST MEASURE 2.7

Key indicators: AI-specific threat modeling, adversarial attack testing, model integrity monitoring, supply chain security for AI components, AI incident detection capability.

---

### D10: Third-Party & Supply Chain Governance

**Measures:** Vendor AI governance requirements, supply chain due diligence, contractual controls, monitoring of third-party AI systems.

**Committee:** Audit | **Standards:** EU AI Act Articles 25-27

Key indicators: Vendor AI governance assessment, contractual AI governance requirements, third-party model evaluation, supply chain transparency, ongoing vendor monitoring.

---

### D11: Stakeholder Impact Layer

**Measures:** External harm accountability: effects on workers, communities, environment, and other stakeholders beyond the company's direct operations.

**Committee:** Full Board | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 14.4, NIST GOVERN 6.1-6.2

Key indicators: Stakeholder impact assessments, workforce transition evidence, environmental footprint transparency, community engagement on AI deployment, human rights due diligence.

*Added in The Alpha Standard v3.*

---

### D12: Corrigibility Assessment

**Measures:** Demonstrated ability to override, shut down, or correct AI systems. Evidence of governance constraining capability deployment.

**Committee:** Risk | **Standards:** EU AI Act Article 14 (Human Oversight), NIST GOVERN 1.1

Key indicators: Documented shutdown or override events, governance hold on deployment, capability restriction evidence, human oversight mechanisms, corrigibility event documentation.

**Weight:** 10% across all profiles. A Corrigibility Event (documented instance where governance overrode a deployment or capability decision) is the highest-weight evidence in the entire framework.

*Added in The Alpha Standard v4.*
